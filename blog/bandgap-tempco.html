Hunting a 3 ppm/degC drift in a bandgap reference.

April 10, 2026 7 min read
  • analog
  • bandgap
  • measurement

The reference read 13 ppm/degC on the bench and 3 ppm/degC in simulation. That gap was large enough to suggest the setup was measuring more than the reference itself.

First suspect: curvature correction

The obvious place to look was the second-order term. A standard Brokaw topology can cancel the linear PTAT and CTAT slopes but still leaves a residual bow over temperature. I plotted simulated and measured output against temperature and the shapes did not match: the measured curve carried a nearly linear tilt on top of the expected parabola.

Second suspect: op-amp offset drift

A linear tilt points naturally to op-amp offset. I swept VOS in simulation and could reproduce a similar family of curves if dVOS/dT was large enough. Trimming offset at room temperature, however, barely changed the slope. That eliminated offset as the dominant error source.

Actual culprit: a thermocouple in the probe

The Kelvin pair on the probe card was not thermally symmetric. One contact path was tungsten on aluminum pad metal, while the return path involved a different junction stack. With the chuck heated and the probe body closer to room temperature, the resulting Seebeck voltage was large enough to masquerade as reference drift.

The circuit was not drifting nearly as much as the measurement path was.

After shorting the Kelvin pair directly at the pad and allowing a much longer dwell at each temperature step, the measured tempco dropped to roughly 4.2 ppm/degC, much closer to simulation.

Lessons

  • Plot error shape, not only the final tempco number.
  • Check for thermal gradients anywhere metal junctions can form.
  • Let the entire fixture settle, not just the DUT chuck temperature.
